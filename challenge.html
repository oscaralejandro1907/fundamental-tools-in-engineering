Design & evaluate a mini electric vehicle

Build · test · measure · defend

Your team designs, builds and evaluates a scale vehicle powered by rechargeable batteries. The goal is a functional prototype capable of moving in a straight line along a track — and a set of numbers that explain why it behaved the way it did.

Brief

What you will actually do

  • Design the vehicle's mechanical and electrical systems as a team.
  • Select and assemble its components against a real budget of mass, space and power.
  • Run experimental tests on a straight track, repeatedly, under controlled conditions.
  • Measure motion and electrical behaviour variables — distance, time, voltage, current.
  • Interpret the results and propose improvements you can justify with your own data.
The part teams underestimate

Building the car is the easy half. The hard half is producing measurements that survive someone else asking "how do you know?"

This module

Where Fundamental Tools fits

The challenge is shared across the block. Fundamental Tools in Engineering is the module that makes your evidence trustworthy. Everything here feeds directly back into the vehicle.

  • Apply basic mathematical tools and concepts to solve the challenge.
  • Use computational tools to manipulate and visualise experimental data.
  • Work through exercises that require interpretation, not just calculation.

The three questions every session asks

  • What did you measure? — the raw quantity, with its unit.
  • How well did you measure it? — precision you can actually defend.
  • So what? — the decision the number changes.
Quantities in play

The variables you will chase

These are the numbers that show up on every telemetry sheet, in every activity, and in your final review. Learn to move between them fluently.

Motion

  • Distance m
  • Time s
  • Average speed m/s
  • Wheel revolutions rev

Electrical

  • Voltage V
  • Current A
  • Power W
  • Energy J or W·h

Quality of evidence

  • Significant figures
  • Uncertainty ±
  • Repeats and scatter
  • Unit consistency
