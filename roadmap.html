Every session ends with something your team can put in the final report. Nothing here is a warm-up exercise — it is all evidence for the vehicle.

Session 01 · ACT-01 · Live

Units, precision, and the cost of getting them wrong

Teams run Mission Control — Vehicle Zero: six rounds of incoming telemetry in mismatched units, two minutes and two attempts each. The flight computer rejects a wrong unit, an unsupported precision, or a bad value — and says which, without giving the answer.

Launch ACT-01 ▸

Run sheet

  • 0–10 Mars Climate Orbiter, 1999. $327M, one unconverted unit.
  • 10–20 Significant figures: what a digit is actually claiming.
  • 20–55 Mission Control, teams of four, one device per team.
  • 55–70 Scores read aloud; debrief the rounds most teams lost.
  • 70–80 Apply it: which vehicle measurements will be hardest to defend?
Takeaway

Precision is a claim about your instrument, not about your calculator.

Session 02 · ACT-02

Track Lab — measuring the vehicle for real

Stopwatch, tape, multimeter. Each team collects its own raw data on a marked track and meets the first uncomfortable fact of experimental work: repeat the run, get a different number.

Produces a clean data table and a distance–time plot.

Session 03 · ACT-03

Power Bench — the electrical budget

Voltage, current, power and energy on the actual drivetrain. Teams predict how long the pack should last, then measure how long it does.

Produces a power budget with a predicted vs. measured column.

Session 04 · ACT-04

Error Hunt — uncertainty, and admitting it

How wrong is a stopwatch reading with a human behind it? How does that error travel through a division? Teams put honest error bars on session 02's plot.

Produces the same plot, now with defensible uncertainty.

Session 05 · ACT-05

Curve Fit — from points to prediction

Fit a straight line to your own track data, read meaning out of the slope, and predict a run nobody has done yet. Then do that run and see how far off you were.

Produces a model, a prediction, and a measured check.

Session 06 · ACT-06

Design Review — defend the numbers

Each team presents its vehicle's telemetry to the room. Other teams may challenge any figure. "We measured it" is not an answer; "we measured it three times with a ±0.2 s stopwatch" is.

Produces the final evidence pack for the challenge.

Throughout

The standing rule

Any number written on a whiteboard, slide or report in this module carries a unit and a precision you can justify. Numbers that do not are simply not accepted.
